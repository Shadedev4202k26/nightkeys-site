Nightkeys

Light your keyboard using your screen.

Nightkeys turns the bottom edge of your display into an adjustable lamp that shines down across your keys — for laptops that ship without a backlit keyboard.

Getting started

  • Nightkeys runs in the menu bar. There is no Dock icon and no window — that's by design.
  • Click the lightbulb icon in the menu bar to open the controls.
  • Toggle the light on and off at the top of the panel.
The effect is only obvious in a dim or dark room. In bright daylight the bar looks like a plain white strip, because your keyboard is already lit.

The controls

Brightness
How much light the bar emits. Maximum output is pure white — every step away from white costs you some brightness.
Temperature
Warm to cool, 1200K to 10000K, on a true blackbody curve. Warmer light is easier on your eyes late at night; cooler light gives you more of it.
Color
Hue and saturation for a tinted light. Deep amber at low brightness preserves night vision better than white. Saturated colors emit noticeably less light.
Bar height
How tall the lit strip is. Taller throws more light but covers more of your screen. Most keyboards are happy somewhere around 20%.
Soft edge
Feathers the top of the bar so it doesn't cut a hard line across whatever you're reading.

Questions

The light disappears when an app goes full screen.

macOS gives full-screen apps their own private Space, and the system does not guarantee that any overlay stays above them. Nightkeys asks to appear in all Spaces and it works in most cases, but some apps — particularly games and video players — can still cover it. There is no public API that fixes this reliably. If you hit it, using the app in a normal window rather than full screen restores the light.

There's no icon in my Dock.

Correct. Nightkeys is a menu bar utility. Look for the lightbulb near your clock.

Does it show up in screenshots or on screen shares?

No. The light bar is deliberately excluded from screen captures and screen sharing, so it won't appear in recordings or on calls.

Does it use more battery?

Slightly, yes. Your display is doing the work of a lamp, and bright pixels cost power. Lower brightness and a shorter bar reduce it.

I have more than one display.

Nightkeys puts a bar on every connected display and adjusts automatically when you plug one in or unplug it.
